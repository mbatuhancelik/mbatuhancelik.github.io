My work previously centered on self-learning structured, interpretable, and expandable representations from continuous sensorimotor data to enable manipulation and long-term planning. Meanwhile, I leveraged active learning to scale up task complexity. 

While I’ve extensively used **robotics as a testbed**, I am eager to apply these principles to tackle complex reasoning tasks—whether in **visual query answering (VQA)**, **reinforcement learning**, **multimodal LLMs**, or **robotics**.

### 💡 The Story Behind the Work

My research motivation was deeply shaped by a specific moment at CoLoRs Lab: watching a colleague build a Lego tower out of hundreds of blocks while our early symbol-discovery models struggled with just a few. My advisor, Dr. Ahmetoğlu, and I looked at that structure and envisioned a robot doing the same—making it our core goal.

After eight months of failed iterations, I traced information flow through our network, identified a representation bottleneck, and proposed the relational module that evolved into **Relational DeepSym** (*RA-L 2024*). 

While theory promised scalability, testing it hit an empirical wall: even 4-object environments required ~1,000,000 samples collected through random exploration. My mathematical analysis showed the probability of discovering an $n$-object interaction scales as $O(1/n!)$, revealing that most collected data consisted of trivial, repetitive interactions. 

My internship at SISReC served as my gateway to cognitive and developmental learning literature, inspiring me to use **active learning** to target high-information interactions. I pitched this intrinsic curiosity approach to Prof. Emre Uğur for my B.S. thesis. The resulting agent successfully scaled to 12-object composite structures—bridging that gap from 4 to 12 objects remains the most fulfilling milestone of my research journey to date, defining the initiative and persistence I bring to scalable AI research.
